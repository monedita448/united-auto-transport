# Dealer-to-Dealer Transfers: How to Ship Trades and Swaps Without Losing the Deal

A customer walks in wanting a specific trim, color or package. You don't have it, but another store does. The trade gets agreed in a phone call. Then the clock starts, because every day the buyer waits is another day they might shop somewhere else.

Here's how to move dealer trades quickly and cleanly.

## Why dealer trades fall apart

Most dealer trades that fail don't fail on price. They fail on timing:

- The unit sits at the other store waiting for a driver
- Nobody owns the transport, so it slips between two desks
- The car arrives with a scratch nobody documented, and now there's a dispute

A transport partner that dispatches fast and documents everything solves all three.

## Documentation: the BOL and condition report

Every unit gets inspected at pickup and again at delivery, and the condition is recorded on the Bill of Lading (BOL). For a dealer trade, that's what keeps both stores on the same page: if something changes in transit, it's documented, and it's the carrier's responsibility, not a disagreement between two dealerships.

Our tip: ask both stores to take a few dated photos at handoff. It takes two minutes and avoids most disputes.

## One-way transfers vs. two-way swaps

**One-way transfer:** you buy or borrow a unit from another store. One pickup, one delivery.

**Two-way swap:** each store sends a unit to the other. We can coordinate both legs so the timing works for both sides, especially when the stores are on the same route.

## When expedited makes sense

Expedited transport costs more, so use it when it protects real money:

- The customer has signed and is waiting on the car
- A competitor has the same unit in stock
- Month-end or a manufacturer program deadline is coming

For routine inventory balancing, standard service is usually the better value.
