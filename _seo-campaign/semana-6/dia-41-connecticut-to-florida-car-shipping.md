# Connecticut to Florida Car Shipping

Once Thanksgiving is over, Connecticut snowbirds start heading south in earnest. Connecticut to Florida is roughly 1,100 to 1,400 miles, most of it on I-95, one of the most congested corridors in the country during the holidays. Shipping your car door-to-door lets you fly down and skip two long days of traffic, tolls and hotels.

## Why Connecticut snowbirds ship instead of drive

- **I-95 holiday traffic.** The stretch through New York, New Jersey, Baltimore and Washington can turn a two-day drive into three.
- **Winter weather.** Early snow and ice on the northern half of the trip add stress before you ever reach the Carolinas.
- **Time with family.** Many snowbirds would rather fly down after the holidays than spend them on the road.
- **Less wear on your car.** Shipping keeps highway miles off the car you'll drive all winter.

## Door-to-door pickup across Connecticut

- **Fairfield County:** pickups in Greenwich, Stamford, Norwalk, Westport and nearby towns are routine. On narrow or private roads, the driver may meet you at a nearby open spot.
- **Hartford and New Haven areas:** we cover the cities and surrounding suburbs.
- **Eastern and northwest Connecticut:** shoreline towns, the Quiet Corner and the Litchfield Hills are covered too. Allow a slightly wider pickup window for rural addresses.

## Open vs. enclosed in winter

Open transport is the most common and affordable choice and works well for daily drivers. In winter, open trailers leaving New England are exposed to road salt and slush, so plan a wash after delivery. Enclosed transport keeps the car fully covered, which makes sense for classic, luxury, low-clearance or high-value vehicles.

## How to prepare

1. Wash the car so existing marks are easy to see at inspection.
2. Take dated photos from every side.
3. Remove personal items, toll tags (like E-ZPass) and garage remotes.
4. Leave about a quarter tank of fuel.
5. Check the battery and tires before a cold-weather pickup.
6. Inspect the car with the driver and sign the Bill of Lading (BOL) at pickup and again at delivery.

## How to book

1. Request a quote with your vehicle, pickup and delivery addresses and preferred dates.
2. Give a pickup window of a few days. Flexibility gets you an FMCSA-verified carrier sooner.
3. Plan around the December holidays, when trucks fill fast.
4. Ask about the return trip. If you're heading back to Connecticut in spring, book that leg early too.
