# Illinois to Florida Car Shipping for Snowbirds

By late November, Illinois winter is settling in and Florida is calling. For many snowbirds, the hardest part of the trip is the car: roughly 1,100 to 1,400 miles of interstate, usually down I-65 and I-75, with hotels, gas and holiday traffic along the way. Shipping your car door-to-door lets you fly down and find it waiting when you arrive.

## Why Illinois snowbirds ship instead of drive

- **Two long days on the road, or more.** Chicago to central Florida is a full two-day drive for most people, and longer to Naples or Miami.
- **Winter weather.** Snow and ice in Illinois, Indiana and Kentucky can make the first half of the trip stressful.
- **Holiday timing.** Many snowbirds want to be settled before Thanksgiving or Christmas, not spending those days behind the wheel.
- **Wear and tear.** Shipping keeps highway miles off a car you plan to drive all winter.

## Pickup across Chicago and Illinois

Chicago is a major carrier hub, which helps on this route.

- **Chicago city:** large carriers can't always reach narrow streets, alleys or downtown buildings. The driver will arrange a nearby open spot, such as a large store parking lot.
- **Suburbs:** pickups in Naperville, Schaumburg, Oak Brook, the North Shore and the western suburbs are routine.
- **Downstate and rural Illinois:** Springfield, Peoria, Champaign and smaller towns are covered too. Allow a slightly wider pickup window.

## Open vs. enclosed in winter

Open transport is the most common and affordable choice and works well for daily drivers. In winter, open trailers are exposed to road salt and slush, so plan to wash the car after delivery. Enclosed transport keeps your car fully covered, which makes sense for classic, luxury, low-clearance or high-value vehicles. ## How to prepare the car

1. Wash the car so existing marks are easy to see at inspection.
2. Take dated photos from every side.
3. Remove personal items, toll tags and garage remotes.
4. Leave about a quarter tank of fuel.
5. Check the battery and tires, since cold weather is hard on both.
6. Inspect the car with the driver and sign the Bill of Lading (BOL) at pickup and again at delivery.

## How to book

1. Request a quote with your vehicle, pickup and delivery addresses and preferred dates.
2. Give a pickup window of a few days if you can. It makes it easier to assign a carrier quickly.
3. Plan around Thanksgiving and the December holidays, when trucks fill fast.
4. Ask about the return trip. If you're coming back to Illinois in spring, book that leg early too.
