# How to Ship a Car You Bought Online From Out of State

Buying a car online is now routine. Buyers browse listings from dealers and private sellers across the country, close the deal from home and then face one question: how do I get the car here? Flying out and driving it back takes days. Shipping it door-to-door with a carrier lets the car come to you.

## Buying a car online from another state

Online car sales open up far more choices than your local lots, often with the exact trim, color or price you want. Common situations:

- **An online dealer or marketplace** with cars stored in another state
- **A traditional dealer** that sells to out-of-state buyers by phone or online
- **A private seller** you found on a listing site
- **An auction purchase** that needs to be picked up from the lot

In each case, the shipping steps are similar. What changes is who hands the car to the driver.

## Dealer vs. private seller: who releases the car

- **Dealers** usually have a set process for releasing cars to carriers. Confirm the contact name, the hours a truck can load and whether the car is fully paid and ready.
- **Private sellers** need to be present (or name someone) to hand over the keys, inspect the car with the driver and sign the Bill of Lading at pickup. Agree on a pickup window that works for them.
- **Auctions and storage lots** may need a release or gate pass. Ask for it before the carrier is dispatched so the driver isn't turned away.

## Documents, keys and payment before pickup

- **Payment completed** and the sale finalized, so the seller is ready to release the car
- **The seller's name, address and phone number** for pickup
- **At least one working key** for the driver
- **Title and registration** handled between you and the seller according to the sale and your state's rules

The carrier needs to know the car is released and where it's going. Title paperwork stays between buyer and seller.

## Open vs. enclosed

Open transport is the most common and affordable option and works for most cars bought online. Enclosed transport keeps the car fully covered, which many buyers choose for luxury, classic, exotic or high-value cars. In winter, plan a wash after an open-transport delivery to remove road salt.

## Inspecting at delivery on the Bill of Lading

At pickup, the driver and the seller inspect the car together and record its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL). Ask the seller for dated photos from pickup too. At delivery:

1. Inspect the car in good light, all the way around.
2. Compare it with the pickup BOL and the seller's photos.
3. Note any new damage on the BOL before signing, and take photos.
4. Keep your copy.

## How to book

1. **Request a quote** with the pickup address, your delivery address and the vehicle.
2. **Share the seller's contact details** and the hours they can meet a driver.
3. **Give a pickup window** of a few days. Flexibility gets you an FMCSA-verified carrier sooner.
4. **Be ready at delivery** to inspect and sign the BOL.

## FAQ

**Can you pick up a car from a private seller?**
Yes. The seller or someone they choose meets the driver, hands over the keys and signs the Bill of Lading at pickup.

**Do I have to be there at pickup?**
No. The seller releases the car. You or someone you trust inspects and signs at delivery.

**How soon can the car ship after I buy it?**
It depends on the route and carrier availability. Booking right after the sale and giving a flexible pickup window helps.
