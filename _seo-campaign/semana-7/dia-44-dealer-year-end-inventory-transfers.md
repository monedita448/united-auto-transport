# Year-End Aged Inventory Transfers for Dealers

December is when many dealer groups take a hard look at aged inventory. A unit that hasn't moved in 60 or 90 days on one lot may sell quickly at a sister store in another market, and dealer trades agreed in November need to be completed before the books close. The catch is timing: the last weeks of the year are also the busiest for carrier capacity.

## Why aged inventory moves in December

- **Year-end goals.** Stores want units where they can sell before December 31.
- **Different markets, different demand.** A truck or SUV that sits in one city can be in demand two states away.
- **Clearing space.** Moving aged units makes room for new model-year arrivals.
- **Floorplan pressure.** Every extra week on the lot is a cost the store carries.

## Sister-store transfers and dealer trades

**Sister-store transfers** move units between rooftops in the same group, often on a regular lane. **Dealer trades** move a unit to another dealer, usually to close a specific deal. Both benefit from a transport partner who knows your locations, contacts and hours. See our guide to [dealer-to-dealer car transport](https://www.united-autotransport.com/blog/dealer-to-dealer-car-transport) for the basics.

## Planning transfers before the holiday weeks

1. **List the units now.** Pull your aged list by store and decide which units move and where.
2. **Batch by lane.** Grouping several units on the same route can make it easier to fill a carrier truck.
3. **Book ahead of the week of Christmas.** Snowbird moves and holiday travel compete for the same trucks.
4. **Give flexible pickup windows.** A window of a few days helps get units assigned to an FMCSA-verified carrier sooner.

## Paperwork, keys and release

- Confirm who releases the unit at the origin store and who receives it at the destination.
- Have keys, and any spare keys that travel with the unit, ready at pickup.
- Share the VIN, stock number and any notes (inoperable, low clearance, oversized) when you book.
- Make sure both stores know the pickup window and the receiving hours, especially around holiday closures.

## Condition reports on the Bill of Lading

Every unit is inspected at pickup and the condition is recorded on the Bill of Lading (BOL). The receiving store checks the unit against that BOL at delivery and notes anything new before signing. Clear photos at both ends protect both stores.

## What to expect from a transport partner

- One point of contact for all your stores.
- Open transport for most units, enclosed for high-end or specialty vehicles.
- Door-to-door pickup and delivery between dealership lots.
- Updates when a unit is assigned, picked up and delivered.
