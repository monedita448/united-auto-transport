# Q4 Inventory Moves: A Year-End Transport Plan for Dealers

The last weeks of the year are when dealer inventory transport gets the hardest. Auction lanes are full, aged units need to move before year-end, retail customers want their cars before the holidays, and northern routes start dealing with snow. Stores that plan Q4 auto transport now keep units moving. Stores that wait end up with cars sitting on lots and customers asking where their vehicle is.

Here's a practical year-end plan for your store.

## Why carrier capacity tightens Nov–Dec

Several things hit the same calendar window:

- **Snowbird season** sends a wave of personal vehicles south, competing for the same truck space on popular lanes
- **Holiday time off** means fewer drivers on the road around Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's
- **Year-end dealer activity** adds auction purchases, dealer trades and aged-unit transfers
- **Winter weather** slows northern routes and can push schedules back

When demand goes up and available trucks go down, pickup windows can stretch. Booking early and staying flexible are the two best tools you have. For the fundamentals of how dealer moves work, see our [dealer auto transport guide](https://www.united-autotransport.com/blog/dealer-auto-transport).

## Aged inventory transfers

Year-end is a natural time to clean up aged units. Moving a car that isn't selling in one market to a store or market where it fits better is often smarter than holding it into the new year.

- **Pull your aged list early.** Decide in early November which units will transfer.
- **Group transfers by lane.** Several units heading the same direction are easier to schedule.
- **Confirm each unit's condition.** Note whether it runs, and flag any low-clearance or high-value cars that may need enclosed transport.
- **Have paperwork ready** at both ends so the carrier isn't left waiting.

## Holiday retail deliveries

A car promised for a holiday gift or year-end purchase is a promise your store made. Protect it by:

- **Booking out-of-state deliveries as soon as the deal is signed**, not the week before the holiday
- **Setting realistic expectations** with the buyer about the delivery window
- **Giving us a reliable contact** at the delivery address, so the driver can coordinate the final stretch
- **Inspecting at delivery.** The buyer, or your representative, should check the car against the Bill of Lading (BOL) before signing

## Weather & northern routes

From late November on, routes through the Upper Midwest, Northeast, Rockies and mountain passes can see snow and ice. Professional carriers drive for conditions, which sometimes means a day or two of delay. Build that cushion into any promise you make to a customer, and consider enclosed transport for units you want protected from road salt and grime.

## Building a weekly transport schedule

The stores that handle Q4 best treat transport like a standing meeting, not an emergency.

1. **Set one weekly cutoff** for submitting units (for example, every Monday).
2. **Send one list** with year, make, model, VIN, pickup and delivery addresses, contacts and running status.
3. **Separate urgent units** like sold cars and auction storage deadlines from flexible ones.
4. **Review last week's moves** and adjust lanes that ran slow.
5. **Plan auction pickups** around storage deadlines. Our [auction car transport guide](https://www.united-autotransport.com/blog/auction-car-transport-for-dealers) covers gate passes and release timing.

A single point of contact and a predictable weekly rhythm make year-end far less stressful.
