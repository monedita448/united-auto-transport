# Corporate Relocation Car Shipping: A Guide for HR and Relocation Managers

When an employee accepts a transfer or a new hire relocates, the household goods get most of the attention. The car often becomes an afterthought until the employee asks, "So how do I get my car there?" A clear corporate vehicle relocation process removes that stress, protects your relocation budget and helps transferees start their new role focused on work, not logistics.

## Why vehicle shipping matters in relocation

For many employees, the car is the second-most valuable thing they move, after their home. Asking them to drive it across the country can mean:

- **Lost workdays** spent on the road instead of starting the new role
- **Safety concerns** on a long solo drive, sometimes in winter
- **Out-of-pocket expenses** for fuel, hotels and meals that later need reimbursing
- **A rough start** for the employee and their family

Including employee car shipping in your relocation package is a practical way to support a smooth transition and a positive first impression of the new role.

## Door-to-door for transferees

Door-to-door service means the carrier picks the car up at or near the employee's current home and delivers it at or near their new one. The transferee doesn't have to drop it off at a terminal or arrange a ride to pick it up.

A few details help it go smoothly:

- **Coordinate with the household move.** Many transferees want the car to arrive shortly after they do.
- **Plan for temporary housing.** If the employee is in a corporate apartment at first, delivery can go there, or to another approved address.
- **Allow for access limits.** If a full-size carrier can't reach a street or complex, the driver will arrange a nearby meeting spot.
- **Designate an adult** at each end to inspect the car and sign the Bill of Lading (BOL).

Most relocations use open transport. Enclosed transport is available for executives with classic, luxury or high-value vehicles. Our [how it works page](https://www.united-autotransport.com/how-it-works.html) walks through each step.

## Billing & documentation

Relocation managers need a process that's easy to approve and easy to reconcile.

- **Clear quotes** based on the actual vehicle, route and dates
- **Consistent documentation**, including the BOL, which records the car's condition at pickup and delivery
- **One point of contact** for questions from HR, the relocation team and the transferee
- **Defined responsibility.** Carriers are responsible for damage that occurs in transit, and their insurance is verified before pickup. [Read about carrier liability](https://www.united-autotransport.com/legal/carrier-liability.html).

Ask us how billing can be set up for your company, whether the employee pays and is reimbursed or your company is billed directly.

## Multi-vehicle households

Families often relocate with two or more vehicles. Shipping them together keeps the move organized and avoids a second road trip. Let us know about every vehicle up front, including any that don't run or are oversized, so we can plan the right carriers and timing.

## Tracking & communication

Transferees are juggling a lot. Clear communication keeps them, and you, from having to chase updates.

- **Confirm pickup windows** directly with the employee
- **Share driver contact details** as pickup and delivery approach
- **Flag schedule changes early**, such as weather or route delays
- **Keep HR in the loop** on the employee's status if your process calls for it

## FAQ

**Can the company book on the employee's behalf?**
Yes. HR or a relocation manager can request the quote and coordinate, with the employee or a designated adult present at pickup and delivery.

Yes. Send us the list of transferees and vehicles, and we'll plan each move.

**How long does a relocation shipment take?**
