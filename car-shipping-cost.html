The Short Answer

For a standard car in good running condition on an open carrier, expect roughly $650 to $2,450 depending on distance: about $900 to $1,100 for a 1,000-mile trip, and about $1,450 to $2,450 for a long cross-country move. Enclosed transport costs more, often about 40% to 150% more. Longer trips cost more in total but less per mile. Motorcycles ship enclosed, typically about $1,100 to $1,500 for 1,000 miles.

Treat every number on this page as an estimate. Shipping prices move with demand, the season, fuel costs and how many trucks are available on your route. The only way to know your price is to get a current quote, and that takes a quick call.

Rates Change With the Time of Year

Car shipping prices are always changing, because they follow demand. When many cars need to move on the same route and trucks are limited, rates go up. When demand slows, they come down. That is why the price you saw last month may not be the price today.

Time of yearWhat happens to demand and price
AutumnSnowbird season begins. Drivers send cars south to Florida, Arizona and other warm-weather states, so southbound routes get busier and more expensive.
WinterSnowbird demand continues on warm-weather destinations. Holidays and winter weather can limit truck availability and stretch schedules.
SpringSnowbirds head home, so northbound routes get busier. The moving season starts to build.
SummerPeak moving season. Some industry estimates put prices roughly 13% to 25% above off-peak levels on busy routes.

What to do about it: if you are shipping during a busy season, plan ahead, and stay flexible on your pickup dates where you can, since flexibility often helps. Read our snowbird guides for when to book and for Arizona.

What Else Affects the Price

  • Distance and route. Busy interstate lanes tend to be cheaper than rural or remote pickups.
  • Open or enclosed. Enclosed protects the car fully and costs more. See open vs. enclosed.
  • Vehicle size. SUVs, pickups and oversized or lifted vehicles take more room and cost more than a sedan.
  • Whether the car runs. A car that doesn't run needs a winch to load, which typically adds about $125 to the regular rate. If it has no wheels or can't roll, it must be loaded and unloaded with a forklift, and you arrange one. See inoperable vehicle shipping.
  • Fuel and truck availability. Both move rates, and both change often.
  • Timing. Rush pickups cost more than flexible ones.
