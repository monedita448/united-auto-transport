New York to Florida Car Shipping: Snowbird Season Guide (2026)

Shipping a car from New York to Florida this winter? Typical transit times, open vs. enclosed options and how to book early. Get a free quote today.

Get a free quote Call now

Every fall, thousands of New Yorkers trade gray skies for Florida sunshine, and a lot of them would rather fly than spend two long days on I-95. That makes New York to Florida one of the busiest car shipping lanes in the country from October through January.

If you're planning to send your car south this season, here's what to expect, from timing and carrier options to how to book without the stress.

Why New York to Florida gets so busy in the fall

Snowbird season concentrates demand into a few months. Retirees, seasonal residents and families with second homes all head south at roughly the same time, and they all need their cars when they arrive.

For you, that means two things:

  • Carrier trucks fill up faster from late October through the holidays.
  • Booking early gives you more pickup-date options, and usually a smoother experience.

Typical transit time and distance

Depending on where you start in New York and where you're going in Florida, the trip is typically about 1,000 to 1,300 miles. Once your car is picked up, transit usually takes about 3 to 5 days.

Your exact timeline depends on:

  • Pickup location (Manhattan and Long Island vs. Upstate)
  • Delivery city (Jacksonville is closer than Naples or Miami)
  • Weather and traffic along the East Coast
  • How flexible your pickup window is

We'll give you a realistic pickup window and delivery estimate before you book, and you'll be able to follow your shipment until delivery.

Open vs. enclosed transport for this route

Open transport is the most common choice on the New York to Florida route. Your car rides on the same type of multi-car carrier dealerships use to move new inventory. It's the most economical option, and carriers on this lane run frequently. Learn more about open auto transport.

Enclosed transport keeps your car inside a covered trailer, protected from road debris and weather. It's the better choice for:

  • Classic and collector cars
  • Exotic or luxury vehicles
  • Low-clearance cars that need liftgate loading
  • Anyone who simply wants maximum protection

See how enclosed auto transport works.

Door-to-door pickup anywhere in New York

With door-to-door service, the carrier picks up as close to your address as is safe and legal, and delivers as close as possible to your Florida home.

A few New York-specific tips:

  • NYC and tight streets: Large carriers can't always fit on narrow residential streets. If that's the case, the driver will arrange to meet you at a nearby open spot, such as a large parking lot.
  • Long Island and Westchester: Usually straightforward pickups. Just make sure there's room for a large truck to stop.
  • Upstate (Buffalo, Rochester, Albany, Syracuse): Plan around early winter weather, and keep your schedule a little flexible.

What affects your price

We don't believe in teaser prices that change after you book. Your quote depends on a few real factors:

  1. Season. Peak snowbird weeks have the highest demand.
  2. Vehicle size and weight. An SUV or pickup takes more space on a carrier than a sedan.
  3. Running condition. Inoperable vehicles need a winch-equipped carrier.
  4. Open vs. enclosed. Enclosed costs more because there are fewer spots per trailer.
  5. Flexibility. A wider pickup window helps us match your car with the right truck.

Be careful with quotes that look far below everyone else's. A price that's too low often means your car sits waiting for a carrier willing to take it.

How to book your New York to Florida shipment

  1. Get your free quote. Tell us your pickup ZIP, delivery ZIP, vehicle and preferred dates.
  2. Confirm your booking. We assign an FMCSA-verified carrier and verify their insurance before pickup.
  3. Pickup and inspection. You and the driver inspect the car together and record its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL).
  4. Track your car while it's on the road.
  5. Delivery and final inspection. Check the car against the BOL before you sign.
