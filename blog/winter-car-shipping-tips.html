Winter Car Shipping: Weather, Delays and How to Prepare

Winter is one of the busiest times to ship a car. Snowbirds head south, families move over the holidays and dealers push year-end inventory, all while snow, ice and short days slow down the roads. Cars still ship safely every day in winter. The key is planning for a little more flexibility and preparing the car for the cold.

How winter weather affects car shipping

  • Snow and ice on northern routes. Storms in the Midwest, the Northeast and the mountain passes out West can close roads or slow traffic for a day or two.
  • Driver safety comes first. Carrier drivers follow federal hours-of-service rules and will wait out dangerous conditions rather than risk your car.
  • Shorter daylight. Fewer daylight hours can make pickups and deliveries in residential areas take longer to schedule.
  • Holiday demand. Thanksgiving through New Year's, carrier trucks on snowbird lanes fill quickly.

Realistic pickup and delivery windows

In winter, plan for a pickup window of a few days rather than one fixed date, and expect delivery estimates to shift by a day or two if a storm hits the route. A few habits help:

  • Book early, ideally two to three weeks ahead during the holidays.
  • Give a flexible pickup window so the first available carrier on your route can take the car.
  • Keep your phone on. The driver will call ahead, and weather may move the timing.
  • Name a backup contact at pickup and delivery in case you're traveling.

A reputable broker gives you a pickup window and an estimated delivery range, not a guaranteed hour.

When to choose enclosed transport

Open transport is the most common and affordable option and works well for most daily drivers, even in winter. Your car will be exposed to road spray and salt, so plan a wash after delivery.

Enclosed transport keeps the car fully covered, which makes sense in winter for:

  • Classic, luxury and exotic cars
  • Low-clearance or high-value vehicles
  • New cars where you want to avoid salt residue

Preparing your car for cold weather

  1. Check the battery. Cold weather is hard on weak batteries, and the car needs to start for loading.
  2. Check tire pressure. Pressure drops in the cold.
  3. Top off antifreeze and windshield washer fluid rated for winter.
  4. Leave about a quarter tank of fuel.
  5. Remove personal items, toll tags and garage remotes.
  6. Wash the car and take dated photos from every side before pickup, so existing marks are easy to see.

What to check at delivery (Bill of Lading)

At pickup, the driver inspects the car with you and records its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL). At delivery:

  • Inspect in good light. If it's dark or snowing, ask to use a flashlight or a covered spot.
  • Compare against the BOL and your pickup photos.
  • Note any new damage on the BOL before signing, and take photos.
  • Keep your copy of the BOL.

Salt and road grime can hide small marks, so take your time.
