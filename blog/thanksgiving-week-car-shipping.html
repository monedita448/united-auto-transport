Car Shipping During Thanksgiving Week: What Changes

Leer en español

Shipping a car during Thanksgiving week? What changes with carriers, pickups and deliveries over the holiday, and how to keep your dates on track.

Get a free quote Call now

Thanksgiving week is one of the busiest weeks of the year on the road. Families travel, snowbirds head south and dealers move inventory ahead of Black Friday. If your car is scheduled to ship this week, it can still move on time. It just helps to know what changes over the holiday and how to keep your dates on track.

Do carriers run on Thanksgiving week?

Yes. Carriers keep running through Thanksgiving week, and many trucks are already loaded and on the road. What changes:

  • Fewer pickups and deliveries on the holiday itself. Many drivers take Thanksgiving Day off or spend it driving between stops rather than loading.
  • Heavier traffic. Wednesday before Thanksgiving and the Sunday after are some of the busiest travel days of the year, which can slow trucks down.
  • Tighter capacity. Snowbird lanes to Florida, Arizona and Texas fill fast, so open spots on a truck go quickly.
  • Weather. Late November can bring the first snow to northern routes and mountain passes.

Pickup and delivery around the holiday

  • Expect a window, not an exact hour. Pickup windows of a few days are normal, and the driver will call ahead.
  • Plan around Thursday. If your window includes Thanksgiving Day, the pickup or delivery may land on Wednesday, Friday or the weekend instead.
  • Watch the weekend after. Many carriers pick back up on Friday and the weekend after Thanksgiving, which is also when Black Friday buyers start booking.
  • Stay reachable. Keep your phone on in case weather or traffic moves the timing.

Who needs to be there (and the Bill of Lading)

Someone has to meet the driver at pickup and at delivery. It doesn't have to be you, but it should be an adult you trust who can:

  • Walk around the car with the driver and agree on its condition
  • Sign the Bill of Lading (BOL) at pickup
  • At delivery, compare the car against the BOL and the pickup photos, note any new damage before signing and keep a copy

If you're traveling for Thanksgiving, name a backup contact such as a neighbor, relative or building manager, and share their phone number when you book.

Tips to keep your dates

  1. Book as early as you can. Every day closer to the holiday means fewer open spots on the trucks.
  2. Be flexible by a day or two. A wider pickup window gets you an FMCSA-verified carrier sooner.
  3. Prepare the car the day before. Remove personal items and toll tags, leave about a quarter tank and take dated photos.
  4. Confirm the address. If a large carrier can't reach your street, agree on a nearby open spot like a store parking lot.
  5. Plan for weather. On northern routes, allow an extra day or two in the delivery estimate.
