Military Holiday Leave and PCS Car Shipping

PCS orders over the holidays or heading home on leave? How to ship your personal vehicle door-to-door, what documents you need and when to book.

PCS orders don't pause for the holidays. Every year, plenty of service members and their families report to a new duty station in December, or fly home on holiday leave and want their own car waiting. Add holiday traffic, winter weather and carrier trucks that fill up fast, and the car can turn into the most stressful part of the move. Here's how to plan it.

PCS moves during the holidays

December PCS moves come with a few extra challenges:

  • Busy carrier lanes. Snowbirds, holiday travelers and dealers are all booking trucks at the same time.
  • Fewer working days. Holiday weeks squeeze the same demand into less time, so pickup windows get tighter.
  • Weather. Snow on northern routes and mountain passes can add a day or two.
  • Family logistics. Report dates, temporary lodging and kids' school breaks all have to line up.

The earlier you book, the more pickup options you have.

When the government ships your POV and when it doesn't

Whether the government ships your personally owned vehicle (POV) depends on your orders. For many overseas moves, one POV may be shipped through the government's vehicle processing program. For most moves within the continental U.S., service members typically drive the vehicle and may be reimbursed under the rules for their move.

Your installation's transportation office is the authority on what your orders cover. Check with them first. Then, if you need to ship a vehicle the government won't move, or you'd rather not drive it across the country during the holidays, a private door-to-door shipment fills the gap. For a full overview, see our PCS military car shipping guide.

Shipping a second vehicle

Many military families own two cars, and orders may only cover one. Common options:

  • Drive one, ship one. One spouse drives the daily car while the second car ships door-to-door.
  • Ship both. If the whole family is flying, ship both cars so you're not doing two long drives.
  • Ship to family first. If housing isn't ready, ship the car to a relative near the new duty station.

Tell us about every vehicle up front, including any that don't run, so we can plan the right carrier.

Documents you'll need

Requirements vary, but have these ready:

  • A copy of your PCS orders, if it helps coordinate dates and addresses
  • Vehicle registration and proof of insurance
  • A working key, plus the name and phone number of the person releasing and receiving the car
  • If someone else hands the car over for you, make sure they're an adult you trust

At pickup and delivery, the driver inspects the car with that person and records its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL). Keep your copy.

Timing around holiday leave

  • Book two to three weeks ahead if you want the car before Christmas, and earlier for coast-to-coast moves.
  • Give a pickup window of a few days. Flexibility gets you a truck sooner.
  • Avoid the holiday itself. Carriers keep running through holiday weeks, but pickups and deliveries on Thanksgiving or Christmas Day are less common.
  • Plan a backup contact. If you're traveling on leave, name someone who can meet the driver.

How to book

  1. Request a quote with pickup and delivery addresses, the vehicle and your report date.
  2. Confirm your pickup window with an FMCSA-verified carrier on your route.
  3. Prepare the car: remove personal items, leave about a quarter tank and note existing marks.
  4. Inspect and sign the BOL at pickup and again at delivery.
