Inoperable Vehicle Transport: Shipping Non-Running Cars for Dealers and Auction Buyers

Not every unit rolls onto a trailer under its own power. Trade-ins with a dead engine, auction cars with mechanical issues, salvage and project vehicles all need a different plan. Inoperable vehicle transport is routine for experienced carriers, as long as the right truck is assigned and the condition is disclosed up front. Here's how non-running car shipping works and how to keep it on schedule.

What counts as inoperable

For transport purposes, a vehicle is usually considered inoperable if it can't be driven onto and off the trailer by the driver. That includes cars that:

  • Won't start or have a dead or missing battery that can't be jumped
  • Start but can't move, due to transmission, drivetrain or brake problems
  • Can't steer properly
  • Have flat, missing or locked wheels
  • Are missing keys

A car that runs but has cosmetic damage is generally still operable. If you're not sure, describe the issue when you book and we'll help you classify it. Getting this right is the single biggest factor in a smooth pickup.

Winch & forklift loading

A non-running car is loaded differently than a running one.

  • Winch loading: Many carriers have a winch that pulls the vehicle up the ramps onto the trailer. This works when the car can roll and steer, even if the engine doesn't run.
  • Forklift loading: Cars that can't roll or steer, such as units with locked wheels or heavy damage, may need a forklift at the pickup or delivery location. Many auctions and salvage yards have one; most homes and small lots don't.

Tell us exactly what the car can and can't do so we assign a carrier with the right equipment. Learn more about our specialized transport options.

Disclosing condition correctly

When you ship a car that doesn't run, be specific. Let us know:

  • Whether it starts, rolls, steers and brakes
  • Whether the wheels and tires are intact and inflated
  • Whether keys will be with the car
  • Any missing parts, heavy damage or leaks
  • Whether a forklift is available at pickup and delivery

If a carrier arrives expecting a running car and finds one that won't roll, the pickup may be delayed or rescheduled. Inoperable units also typically cost more to ship because of the extra equipment and handling, so accurate details keep your quote accurate too. As always, the car's condition is recorded on the Bill of Lading (BOL) at pickup.

Auction salvage units

Auction buyers move a lot of non-running inventory. Salvage and damaged units often come with storage deadlines, gate pass requirements and release paperwork, so timing matters.

  • Book as soon as you win the unit, so the carrier can reach it before storage fees start
  • Confirm the release and gate pass are ready before the driver arrives
  • Ask whether the yard provides forklift loading
  • Check pickup hours, since many yards have limited loading windows

Timing

Fewer carriers on any given lane have winch equipment, so pickup windows for inoperable units can be a bit wider than for running cars. Flexible dates help. During busy periods like year-end, book as early as you can.
