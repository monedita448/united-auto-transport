How to Ship a Car as a Christmas Gift

Leer en español

Giving a car for Christmas to someone in another state? How to ship it door-to-door, keep the surprise, choose open or enclosed, and who signs the Bill of Lading. Free quote.

A car is one of the biggest gifts anyone can give, and more often than not the person getting it lives in another state. A son or daughter at college, a parent who just retired, a grandchild with a new license. You don't have to drive it across the country yourself. A carrier can pick the car up and deliver it door-to-door, so it is waiting in the driveway when the holidays arrive.

Why shipping beats driving the gift yourself

  • No long December drive. Holiday traffic and winter weather make a multi-day road trip stressful.
  • No extra miles on the car. The odometer stays close to where it was at pickup, which matters for a new or nearly new car.
  • You stay home for the holidays. Instead of flying back after dropping the car off, you book the shipment and track it.
  • It works for dealer purchases too. If you bought the car from a dealer in another state, the carrier can pick it up right at the dealership.

When to book for Christmas delivery

Mid-December is one of the busiest stretches of the year for carriers, with snowbirds heading south, dealers moving year-end inventory and families shipping cars home. To have the car there before Christmas:

  • Book two to three weeks ahead, earlier for coast-to-coast routes.
  • Give a pickup window of a few days. Flexibility gets you an FMCSA-verified carrier sooner.
  • Plan delivery for a few days before the 25th, not Christmas Eve, so a weather or traffic delay doesn't cut it close.

Our holiday car shipping deadlines guide has a simple booking timeline for the season.

Keeping the surprise

A surprise is part of the fun, but someone still has to meet the driver. A few ways to make it work:

  • Name a trusted adult at delivery, such as a spouse, roommate, neighbor or family member who is in on the surprise.
  • Deliver to a nearby address, like a relative's home, and move the car to the recipient's driveway on the big day.
  • Share the driver's call with your helper. The driver calls before arriving, so make sure that number belongs to someone who knows the plan.

Open or enclosed for a gift car

Open transport is the most common and affordable option and works well for most cars. In winter, open trailers are exposed to road salt and spray, so plan a quick wash before the reveal. Enclosed transport keeps the car fully covered, which many people choose for a new, luxury or classic car. Compare both in our open vs. enclosed guide.

Pickup, delivery and the Bill of Lading

At pickup, the driver inspects the car with whoever hands it over (you, a seller or the dealer) and records its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL). At delivery, the person receiving it should:

  1. Inspect the car in good light, all the way around.
  2. Compare it with the pickup BOL and any photos from pickup.
  3. Note any new damage on the BOL before signing.
  4. Keep a copy.
