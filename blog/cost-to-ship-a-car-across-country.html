How Much Does It Cost to Ship a Car Across the Country? (What Really Moves the Price)

"How much does it cost to ship a car?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on your car, your route and your timing. There's no single flat price for cross-country shipping. Instead, a handful of car shipping price factors combine to set your quote. Once you understand them, you can make smart choices and spot a quote that doesn't add up.

The main price factors

The cost to ship a car across the country comes down to six things:

  1. Distance and how popular your route is
  2. Your vehicle's size, weight and condition
  3. Open or enclosed transport
  4. The time of year
  5. How flexible your dates are
  6. Pickup and delivery locations

Let's walk through each one.

Distance & route popularity

Longer trips cost more overall, but distance isn't the whole story. Carriers price by lane. Busy routes between major metros, like a California to New York car shipping run, have lots of trucks moving in both directions, which keeps them competitive. Pickups or deliveries in remote or rural areas may cost more because a carrier has to leave its main route to reach you.

Direction matters too. When many people are heading the same way at the same time, that direction is in higher demand.

Vehicle size & condition

A carrier trailer has limited space and weight capacity. A large SUV, full-size pickup or van takes up more room than a compact sedan, so it generally costs more to ship. Lifted trucks, oversized tires and roof racks can also affect the price.

Condition matters as well. A car that doesn't run needs a winch-equipped carrier and extra handling time, so inoperable vehicles typically cost more than running ones. Always tell us the truth about condition when you book. It keeps your quote accurate and avoids surprises at pickup.

Open vs. enclosed

Open transport is the most common and most economical option. Enclosed transport protects your car from weather and road debris and is ideal for classic, exotic and luxury vehicles, but it costs more because enclosed trucks carry fewer cars and there are fewer of them on the road. Read our open vs. enclosed car transport guide to decide which fits your car.

Season (snowbird peak)

Demand changes through the year. In fall, snowbirds send cars south to Florida, Arizona and Texas; in spring, those cars head back north. Summer is a busy season for relocations and college moves. Peak demand on a lane can raise prices and stretch pickup windows, while quieter periods can work in your favor. Winter weather on northern routes can also affect scheduling.

Flexibility saves money

This is the factor you control most. A wider pickup window gives us more carriers to choose from, which can mean a better price and a smoother match. If your dates are firm, expedited service may be available, usually at a higher cost. Booking early, especially before peak season, also helps.

Red flags: too-low quotes

A quote far below everyone else's should make you cautious, not excited. Watch for:

  • A price that changes after you book, or a "deposit" that rises once a carrier is assigned
  • No clear terms on what's included and when you pay
  • Vague answers about carrier licensing and insurance
  • Pressure to decide on the spot

An unrealistically low price often means no carrier will accept the load, and your car simply waits. Ask how carriers are vetted and whether their insurance is verified before pickup.
