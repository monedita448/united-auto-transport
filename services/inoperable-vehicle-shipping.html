Can You Ship a Car That Doesn't Run?

Yes. Inoperable vehicle shipping is the same door-to-door service as standard auto transport, with one difference: because the car can't be driven onto the trailer, the carrier loads it with a winch. Only some carriers are equipped to do that, so we match your vehicle with one that is, instead of leaving you to call around.


A vehicle counts as inoperable when it can't be driven onto and off the carrier under its own power. That includes a car that won't start or has no key. Project cars, auction and salvage purchases, vehicles waiting on repairs, and cars that broke down far from home are all common.


You can ship a non-running car on an open carrier, and many open carriers are equipped with winches and rollback ramps. If your vehicle is a classic or high-value project and you want it covered in transit, ask a specialist about enclosed transport.

What Does It Cost to Ship a Non-Running Car?

Winch loading: about $125

Loading a non-running vehicle with a winch typically adds about $125 to your regular transport rate. That is on top of the price for your route, not instead of it.

Your regular rate depends on distance, vehicle size, route demand and time of year. A quick call gets you a real, current-market price for your vehicle and route, and your specialist will confirm exactly what applies to your car.

No Wheels, or the Car Can't Roll? Plan for a Forklift

A winch can only pull a vehicle that rolls. If your vehicle has no wheels or can't roll, it has to be loaded onto the carrier and unloaded again with a forklift.

You are responsible for arranging a forklift, at pickup to load the vehicle and at delivery to unload it. Any available forklift will do. Without one, we can't send a carrier to pick it up.

Tell your specialist about it when you call, and we'll walk you through what's needed at pickup and delivery so the carrier doesn't make a wasted trip.

Tell Us Before Pickup

The more accurately we know your vehicle's condition, the more accurate your price and carrier match will be. Mention anything on this list when you call:

  • Whether the engine starts
  • Whether you have a key
  • Flat tires or missing wheels, and whether the car rolls freely (if it can't roll, you'll need a forklift, see above)
  • Body or underbody damage, or anything hanging loose
  • Very low ground clearance
  • Where it's parked (home, repair shop, dealership, auction lot, tow yard)
